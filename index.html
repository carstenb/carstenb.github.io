What I work on

Four things I want to build more of

Platform as a product

Capabilities teams choose to use

Shared platforms create value when teams want to use them, not simply because they have to. That means treating internal developers as real users: understanding their problems, creating a roadmap around them, measuring adoption and enabling teams instead of becoming their gatekeeper.

APIs, MCP & ecosystems

The surface others build on

APIs are products with users, lifecycle decisions and a developer experience of their own. MCP adds a new kind of consumer: AI agents. I'm interested in how reusable product capabilities can serve developers, partners and machines without creating another layer of special-case integrations.

Product strategy

Where to invest, and why now

Direction that still makes sense when explained to a customer, an engineering lead and a board — including where to invest, what to stop doing and why the trade-offs are worth it.

Team topologies & org design

Organizations shaped for flow

Team boundaries shape the systems we build. I care about ownership models that reduce cognitive load and coordination, let stream-aligned teams move independently and keep platform teams from becoming another dependency.

Outside the job

Things I build on the side

I like building things outside my day job — sometimes around a problem worth solving, sometimes simply to understand an idea by making it real. AI tools have changed how far I can take those ideas myself; I increasingly use them across product thinking, design, prototyping and implementation. Not everything needs to become a company.

Building · Mandoria

Making care bureaucracy bearable

Care, health and insurance processes often turn the affected person into the integration layer between doctors, insurers, care services, suppliers and authorities — repeatedly carrying the same information from one process into the next.

Mandoria won't abolish that system. It brings information, processes, deadlines, documents and forms into one place, so more of the organizational load can sit with software instead of the person navigating it.

I'm building it solo with AI deeply embedded in the process — using tools such as Claude across product thinking, design and implementation. Part of the experiment is understanding how much of a real product one product person can now build independently.

AI-assisted builds · experiments

Building to understand

I use small builds to explore ideas quickly — websites, product prototypes, AI workflows and tools that may never need to become full products.

Claude and other AI tools are increasingly part of that process, shortening the distance between an idea, a product decision and something I can actually use.
